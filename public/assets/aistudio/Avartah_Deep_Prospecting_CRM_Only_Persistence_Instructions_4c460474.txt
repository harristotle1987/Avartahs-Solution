GOOGLE AI STUDIO IMPLEMENTATION ADDENDUM
Baseline project: avartah-solutions (14) (3).zip
This addendum supersedes any earlier instruction that says to save qualified/discovered prospects automatically.

NON-NEGOTIABLE BUSINESS RULE
The Prospecting Engine is a temporary research workspace, NOT a database ingestion pipeline.
Search, discovery, deep contact research, website assessment, qualification, filtering, sorting and review must NOT insert or update prospect records in Neon.
Only the explicit administrator action "Add to CRM" may persist a selected prospect into the existing authoritative CRM tables.
If the administrator does not add a result to CRM, its prospect data must not be saved to Neon.

3. CHANGE DISCOVERY TO EPHEMERAL RESULTS
Refactor discovery so that:
- /api/prospects/locate performs discovery and enrichment only.
- It must not INSERT, UPDATE or DELETE prospects, prospects_excluded, CRM leads, or any other persistent business/prospect table.
- /api/prospects/search must either be deprecated safely or delegate to the same non-persisting discovery service. It must not independently save raw candidates.
- /api/prospects/batch must no longer be used for automatic discovery persistence. Replace or repurpose it only if the CRM workflow requires an explicit user-confirmed bulk Add to CRM action.
- Remove automatic writes to prospects_excluded for no-contact or rejected candidates. Keep those records in temporary search results only.
- Do not create a raw_leads, staging_leads, discovery_results or temporary prospect table in Neon.
- Do not persist search result payloads, rejected businesses, duplicates, contact lookups, provider responses, website crawl output or search history in Neon.
- Do not write per-search result snapshots to Neon.
- Do not silently persist results in another backend, local database, object store or analytics table.
- Return results to the frontend and hold them in the existing React state for the active page/session. If a multi-request search needs continuation, keep the cursor and accumulated candidate data in the active client session or a bounded server job mechanism that does not persist candidate records in Neon.
- If the page is refreshed or closed, temporary search results may be lost. This is acceptable and should be clearly communicated; persistence occurs only through Add to CRM.
- Do not put full contact datasets into localStorage as a workaround. Prefer in-memory React state. sessionStorage may only be considered for a small, short-lived cursor/job token if strictly necessary, not the full prospect dataset.

4. READ-ONLY DUPLICATE CHECKING
It is acceptable to read existing CRM data to identify duplicates, but discovery must not write to Neon.
- Use a lightweight read-only matching endpoint or one batched query against existing CRM records.
- Do not fetch the entire prospects table on every batch.
- Match using normalized domain, phone, email, and business name + location, with a safe hierarchy.
- Return only the minimum match status/CRM record identifier needed by the UI.
- Never create a placeholder CRM record to reserve a discovered business.
- Do not update existing CRM contact details during discovery.
- A candidate already in CRM should be marked "Already in CRM" and excluded from new CRM admission unless the administrator explicitly chooses an authorized update workflow.

5. DEEP CONTACT RESEARCH — BUSINESSES WITHOUT WEBSITES
The target is small businesses with NO official website, not businesses that already have good websites.
Search for genuine businesses in the selected category and location, then investigate their publicly available contact footprint.

For each candidate:
A. Establish business identity:
- Business name and category.
- Physical location/address and locality.
- Public phone number, where available.
- Public business listing/profile URLs.
- Discovery source and source URL.
- Evidence that the business is active and genuine.

B. Determine web presence:
- No official website.
- Social-media-only presence.
- Directory/listing-only presence.
- Broken or unreachable official website.
- Existing official website (exclude by default).
Do not mistake a Facebook/Instagram profile, Google Maps listing, directory page or WhatsApp link for an official website.

C. Deep contact discovery:
When no official website exists, do NOT stop contact research. Search permitted public sources such as:
- Search engine results/snippets for the exact business name + location + phone/email/contact.
- Public business directories and chamber/association listings.
- Public Facebook business pages and public Instagram business profiles, where access is permitted.
- Public LinkedIn company pages where relevant.
- Public booking, menu, delivery or marketplace profiles that identify the business.
- Publicly accessible business registry or professional association pages where relevant.
- Public map/business listings and their published phone numbers.
- Public pages linked from the business's own public profiles.

Use query variations carefully, for example:
- "<business name>" "<city>" email
- "<business name>" contact
- "<business name>" phone
- "<business name>" WhatsApp
- "<business name>" Facebook
- "<business name>" Instagram
- "<business name>" bookings/enquiries
Do not run every variation against every search provider. Use a staged process and stop when reliable contact information is found or the bounded source budget is exhausted.

D. If a business has an official website:
- Crawl the homepage and a small set of likely contact pages: /contact, /contact-us, /about, /about-us, /enquiries, /booking.
- Extract visible public email addresses, mailto links, phone numbers, JSON-LD LocalBusiness data, and public social links.
- Decode common publicly visible email obfuscation where lawful and technically safe.
- Do not crawl an unlimited number of pages. Use a small page cap, same-domain restrictions, response-size limits and timeouts.

E. If a business has NO official website:
- Search public web evidence and public business profiles for a contact email or phone.
- A phone number found on a public listing may be useful even when no email exists.
- Do not claim an email was extracted from a website when the business has no website.
- Do not invent likely emails such as info@domain, bookings@domain or name@gmail.com.
- Do not treat AI-generated email guesses or domain permutations as found or verified contacts.
- If no public email can be found, leave email blank and show "Not found".
- If phone exists but email does not, keep the candidate visible as "Phone available — email not found".
- If neither phone nor email nor another usable public business contact exists, show it under "Needs contact research" or exclude it from CRM-ready results; do not fabricate contact data.

6. CONTACT EVIDENCE AND VERIFICATION LABELS
For every email and phone, retain provenance in the temporary result:
- value
- source URL/provider
- source type (official site, public directory, public social profile, public map listing, search result)
- date/time checked
- evidence excerpt or location on page, where appropriate
- status

Use distinct statuses:
- FOUND: publicly observed on a source.
- FORMAT_VALID: syntax/number format is valid.
- SOURCE_CONFIRMED: the source explicitly associates it with the business.
- INDEPENDENTLY_VERIFIED: validated through a permitted independent verification method.
Never label an email "verified" solely because an LLM suggested it, a regex found it, or its format is valid.
Do not perform intrusive SMTP probing. Do not send test emails or WhatsApp messages.
For WhatsApp, only show a WhatsApp action when a usable phone number exists and the link is correctly formatted; do not claim the account is active unless that has been checked through an approved method.

8. RECOMMENDED DISCOVERY/ENRICHMENT ARCHITECTURE
Use a staged pipeline:
Stage 1 — Business discovery:
- Use the existing permitted business discovery sources first.
- Retrieve business identity, category, location, listing URL, phone and website presence.
- Use pagination/cursors and deduplicate in memory.
- Do not persist raw results.

Stage 2 — Website-presence classification:
- Determine whether the business has an official website.
- Default target: no official website.
- Social-only and directory-only presence count as no official website.
- Exclude good official websites by default.
- Do not assume a missing website field proves no website; perform a bounded verification/search.

Stage 3 — Contact research:
- For no-website businesses, use a limited set of public search and directory sources to find public phone/email/social contact evidence.
- For businesses with a website, crawl only a small set of same-domain public pages.
- Run enrichment only for candidates that passed identity and website-opportunity checks.
- Do not repeat the same search queries or crawl the same URLs within the same job.

Stage 4 — Qualification:
- Require genuine business evidence.
- Prioritise no-website businesses.
- Score contact completeness and website opportunity separately.
- Show the source and confidence for each contact.
- Do not lower standards to force a target count.

Stage 5 — Temporary result review:
- Return enriched candidates to the Prospecting page.
- Keep candidates, evidence, scores and filters in client memory for the active session.
- Allow sort, filter, inspect, export and select without database writes.
- Clearly show "Temporary search results — not saved to CRM".
- Provide an explicit Add to CRM action for each result and a bulk Add Selected to CRM action.

Stage 6 — Explicit CRM persistence:
- Only when the administrator clicks Add to CRM (or confirms Add Selected) may the backend persist records.
- Submit only the selected candidate records to a protected admin endpoint.
- Validate and normalize all fields server-side.
- Recheck duplicates immediately before insert.
- Insert into the existing authoritative CRM/prospects data model in a transaction.
- Do not create a separate prospect staging table.
- Use idempotency/unique constraints so double-clicks and retries do not create duplicates.
- Return per-record results: added, already exists, rejected/invalid, failed.
- Never save unselected candidates or rejected candidates.
- Do not automatically promote every discovered candidate.

11. PROVIDER RATE LIMITS, COST AND RESPONSIBLE ACCESS
- Use a primary provider first; use fallback only when necessary.
- Set low bounded concurrency (initially 2–3).
- Cache query/geocoding results in memory for the active job.
- Avoid repeated Nominatim/Overpass requests and respect their public usage policies.
- Respect search-provider terms, robots/access restrictions and rate limits.
- Use bounded timeouts, Retry-After, exponential backoff and circuit breakers.
- Do not use rotating proxies, browser fingerprint tricks or mirror rotation to evade limits or blocks.
- Do not scrape login-only, private, paywalled or access-controlled data.
- Do not bypass CAPTCHAs or access controls.
- Apply same-domain restrictions and SSRF protections to all website crawling.
- Never run unlimited concurrent crawls.

12. SEARCH TARGET AND STOP CONDITIONS
- Default qualified target: 20.
- Continue through additional bounded batches until 20 qualified businesses are available, sources are exhausted, the candidate budget is reached, the administrator stops, or provider limits prevent continuation.
- Initial raw candidate ceiling: 500 per search.
- Do not increase request volume merely to hit 20.
- Count only unique businesses with genuine public contact evidence and a real website opportunity as qualified.
- Keep separate totals: raw found, unique, no official website, public phone found, public email found, contact-qualified, already in CRM, excluded, and provider errors.
- Do not persist these counters in Neon. Keep them in current job/session state.
